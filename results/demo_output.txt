  QUERY Q1: Total sales per product category across all regions
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 1  Global query (relational algebra)
      GAMMA category; SUM(qty*unit_price) ( SIGMA status<>'CANCELLED' (ORDERS JOIN ORDER_ITEM JOIN PRODUCT) )
  STEP 2  Decomposition: normalise, analyse, simplify
      relations used : ORDERS, ORDER_ITEM, PRODUCT
      join graph     : 2 equi-join(s) (order_id / prod_id / warehouse_id), no cross products
      simplification : redundant predicates removed, query is conjunctive and valid
  STEP 3  Data localization (using the data dictionary)
      ORDERS = ORDERS_N U ORDERS_S U ORDERS_W
      ORDER_ITEM = ORDER_ITEM_N U ORDER_ITEM_S U ORDER_ITEM_W
      PRODUCT = PRODUCT_R  (replicated at every site -> read the local copy)
  STEP 4  Reduction
      no selection on the fragmentation attribute -> all sites must be contacted
      join reduction: ORDERS_i JOIN ORDER_ITEM_j = empty for i<>j (derived fragmentation),
      so 3 local joins replace 9 fragment joins
      note: SUM is distributive: each site returns a partial SUM per category, the coordinator adds them.
  STEP 5  Optimisation: push selections, joins and partial aggregation to the sites;
          ship only small partial results to the coordinator
  STEP 6  Execution (one thread per site, in parallel)
      -- site North (ddbms_north): 4 row(s) in 14.8 ms
        SELECT p.category, SUM(oi.qty * oi.unit_price)
        FROM orders o
        JOIN order_item oi ON oi.order_id = o.order_id
        JOIN product p ON p.prod_id = oi.prod_id
        WHERE o.status <> 'CANCELLED'
        GROUP BY p.category
      -- site West (ddbms_west): 5 row(s) in 14.3 ms
        SELECT p.category, SUM(oi.qty * oi.unit_price)
        FROM orders o
        JOIN order_item oi ON oi.order_id = o.order_id
        JOIN product p ON p.prod_id = oi.prod_id
        WHERE o.status <> 'CANCELLED'
        GROUP BY p.category
      -- site South (ddbms_south): 3 row(s) in 14.4 ms
        SELECT p.category, SUM(oi.qty * oi.unit_price)
        FROM orders o
        JOIN order_item oi ON oi.order_id = o.order_id
        JOIN product p ON p.prod_id = oi.prod_id
        WHERE o.status <> 'CANCELLED'
        GROUP BY p.category
  STEP 7  Combine partial results at the coordinator
      +-------------+-------------+
      | category    | total_sales |
      +-------------+-------------+
      | Clothing    | 19984.00    |
      | Books       | 14380.00    |
      | Electronics | 13196.00    |
      | Grocery     | 12830.00    |
      | Home        | 12579.00    |
      +-------------+-------------+

  QUERY Q2: Average order value per region (and overall)
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 1  Global query (relational algebra)
      GAMMA region; SUM(total), COUNT(order_id) ( ORDERS JOIN ORDER_ITEM )  then  AVG = SUM / COUNT
  STEP 2  Decomposition: normalise, analyse, simplify
      relations used : ORDERS, ORDER_ITEM
      join graph     : 1 equi-join(s) (order_id / prod_id / warehouse_id), no cross products
      simplification : redundant predicates removed, query is conjunctive and valid
  STEP 3  Data localization (using the data dictionary)
      ORDERS = ORDERS_N U ORDERS_S U ORDERS_W
      ORDER_ITEM = ORDER_ITEM_N U ORDER_ITEM_S U ORDER_ITEM_W
  STEP 4  Reduction
      no selection on the fragmentation attribute -> all sites must be contacted
      join reduction: ORDERS_i JOIN ORDER_ITEM_j = empty for i<>j (derived fragmentation),
      so 3 local joins replace 9 fragment joins
      note: AVG is NOT distributive: averaging per-site averages is wrong.
      note: Rewrite AVG as (SUM, COUNT) at each site and divide at the coordinator.
  STEP 5  Optimisation: push selections, joins and partial aggregation to the sites;
          ship only small partial results to the coordinator
  STEP 6  Execution (one thread per site, in parallel)
      -- site North (ddbms_north): 1 row(s) in 11.1 ms
        SELECT o.region, SUM(oi.qty * oi.unit_price), COUNT(DISTINCT o.order_id)
        FROM orders o
        JOIN order_item oi ON oi.order_id = o.order_id
        WHERE o.status <> 'CANCELLED'
        GROUP BY o.region
      -- site West (ddbms_west): 1 row(s) in 11.0 ms
        SELECT o.region, SUM(oi.qty * oi.unit_price), COUNT(DISTINCT o.order_id)
        FROM orders o
        JOIN order_item oi ON oi.order_id = o.order_id
        WHERE o.status <> 'CANCELLED'
        GROUP BY o.region
      -- site South (ddbms_south): 1 row(s) in 10.8 ms
        SELECT o.region, SUM(oi.qty * oi.unit_price), COUNT(DISTINCT o.order_id)
        FROM orders o
        JOIN order_item oi ON oi.order_id = o.order_id
        WHERE o.status <> 'CANCELLED'
        GROUP BY o.region
  STEP 7  Combine partial results at the coordinator
      +--------+--------+----------+-----------------+
      | region | orders | total    | avg_order_value |
      +--------+--------+----------+-----------------+
      | N      | 8      | 27801.00 | 3475.12         |
      | S      | 7      | 14564.00 | 2080.57         |
      | W      | 7      | 30604.00 | 4372.00         |
      | ALL    | 22     | 72969.00 | 3316.77         |
      +--------+--------+----------+-----------------+

  QUERY Q3: Orders above a threshold in region N only
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 1  Global query (relational algebra)
      SIGMA total > X ( GAMMA order_id; SUM(qty*unit_price) ( SIGMA region='N' (ORDERS) JOIN ORDER_ITEM ) )   [region = 'N']
  STEP 2  Decomposition: normalise, analyse, simplify
      relations used : ORDERS, ORDER_ITEM
      join graph     : 1 equi-join(s) (order_id / prod_id / warehouse_id), no cross products
      simplification : redundant predicates removed, query is conjunctive and valid
  STEP 3  Data localization (using the data dictionary)
      ORDERS = ORDERS_N U ORDERS_S U ORDERS_W
      ORDER_ITEM = ORDER_ITEM_N U ORDER_ITEM_S U ORDER_ITEM_W
  STEP 4  Reduction
      fragment elimination: predicate region='N' contradicts -> removed 4: ORDERS_S, ORDERS_W, ORDER_ITEM_S, ORDER_ITEM_W
      join reduction: ORDERS_i JOIN ORDER_ITEM_j = empty for i<>j (derived fragmentation),
      so 1 local joins replace 1 fragment joins
      note: The predicate region='N' contradicts the predicates of ORDERS_W and ORDERS_S, so those fragments are eliminated.
  STEP 5  Optimisation: push selections, joins and partial aggregation to the sites;
          ship only small partial results to the coordinator
  STEP 6  Execution (one thread per site, in parallel)
      -- site North (ddbms_north): 4 row(s) in 8.5 ms
        SELECT o.order_id, o.cust_id, SUM(oi.qty * oi.unit_price) AS total
        FROM orders o
        JOIN order_item oi ON oi.order_id = o.order_id
        GROUP BY o.order_id, o.cust_id
        HAVING SUM(oi.qty * oi.unit_price) > %s
  STEP 7  Combine partial results at the coordinator

  QUERY Q4: Low-stock items across all warehouses
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 1  Global query (relational algebra)
      PI city,prod_id,name,stock ( SIGMA stock < reorder_level (INVENTORY JOIN PRODUCT JOIN WAREHOUSE) )
  STEP 2  Decomposition: normalise, analyse, simplify
      relations used : INVENTORY, PRODUCT, WAREHOUSE
      join graph     : 2 equi-join(s) (order_id / prod_id / warehouse_id), no cross products
      simplification : redundant predicates removed, query is conjunctive and valid
  STEP 3  Data localization (using the data dictionary)
      INVENTORY = INVENTORY_N U INVENTORY_S U INVENTORY_W
      PRODUCT = PRODUCT_R  (replicated at every site -> read the local copy)
      WAREHOUSE = WAREHOUSE_R  (replicated at every site -> read the local copy)
  STEP 4  Reduction
      no selection on the fragmentation attribute -> all sites must be contacted
      note: PRODUCT and WAREHOUSE are replicated, so the joins are evaluated locally with no data shipping.
  STEP 5  Optimisation: push selections, joins and partial aggregation to the sites;
          ship only small partial results to the coordinator
  STEP 6  Execution (one thread per site, in parallel)
      -- site North (ddbms_north): 2 row(s) in 10.9 ms
        SELECT w.city, i.prod_id, p.name, i.stock, i.reorder_level
        FROM inventory i
        JOIN product p ON p.prod_id = i.prod_id
        JOIN warehouse w ON w.warehouse_id = i.warehouse_id

      -- site West (ddbms_west): 1 row(s) in 10.9 ms

        FROM inventory i
        JOIN product p ON p.prod_id = i.prod_id
        JOIN warehouse w ON w.warehouse_id = i.warehouse_id
        WHERE i.stock < i.reorder_level
      -- site South (ddbms_south): 2 row(s) in 10.7 ms
        SELECT w.city, i.prod_id, p.name, i.stock, i.reorder_level
        FROM inventory i
        JOIN product p ON p.prod_id = i.prod_id
        JOIN warehouse w ON w.warehouse_id = i.warehouse_id

  SCENARIO 4b  WITH strict two-phase locking (SELECT ... FOR UPDATE)

     0.01s  [T1] order 30101 created at South for customer 3001
     0.01s  [T1] P012: South warehouse has 1 (row locked), need 1
     0.42s  [T2] order 30102 created at South for customer 3002
     2.03s  [T1] P012 x1 will ship from WH-S (South)
     2.03s  [T1] 2PC PHASE 1 (voting): PREPARE sent to ['South']

     2.03s  [T1] 2PC PHASE 2: sending COMMIT to participants
     2.03s  [T2] P012: South warehouse has 0 (row locked), need 1
     2.03s  [T1] COMMITTED
     2.04s  [T2] P012: North warehouse has 0 (row locked), need 1
     2.05s  [T2] P012: West warehouse has 0 (row locked), need 1
     2.06s  [T2] ABORTED (insufficient stock for P012 in every warehouse) - all local branches rolled back

  Outcome:
    customer 3001: order 30101 CONFIRMED
    customer 3002: REJECTED (insufficient stock for P012 in every warehouse)
    +--------------------+
    | units_sold_of_p012 |
    +--------------------+
    | 1                  |
    +--------------------+
    Physical stock of P012 at South:
    +-------+
    | stock |
    +-------+
    | 0     |
    +-------+
  >>> CORRECT: exactly 1 unit sold, no overselling.

  Why it works: locks are held until the 2PC commit finishes (strict 2PL), so the
  second buyer waits, then re-reads stock = 0 and is rejected. Schedules are serialisable.

  SCENARIO 6  Crash and recovery  (Syllabus 2.3)
